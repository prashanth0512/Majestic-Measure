OUR STORY

The History of
Majestic Measure

Born from a passion for hand-craftsmanship and a belief that quality endures.

In the autumn of 2015, a 29-year-old Edward Maison left his position as a senior cutter at John Huntsman and signed the lease on a small first-floor suite at 42 Savile Row. He had £8,000 in savings, a hand-built cutter's table, and an unshakeable belief that there was still a place in the world for garments made entirely by hand.

The first year was quiet. Edward cut his first solo suits for three clients a barrister, a jazz musician, and a retired admiral each of whom became a client for life. Word spread slowly, as it does among people who value discretion. By 1992, the suite was full.

Today, Majestic Measure employs seven master tailors, a fabric curator, and a small team of dedicated support staff. We make fewer than 200 garments per year deliberately. Quality is incompatible with volume.

OUR VISION 👁‍🗨

To Keep Craft Alive in an Age of Convenience

We live in an era that prizes speed over permanence. The ability to produce a garment of genuine quality measured, cut, and sewn by hand is a knowledge that must be actively preserved, taught, and valued.

Our apprenticeship programme trains the next generation of master tailors. Our work is our argument: that slowness, when deliberate, is a form of respect for the client, the cloth, and the craft.

"We are not in the clothing business. We are in the time business."

William Cross, Senior Tailor
